What it does

You need two LoRa mesh nodes (radios like a Heltec V3 or a T1000-E). One stays at a base. You carry the other, connected to your phone over Bluetooth (the phone itself has no LoRa radio). Meshurement™ logs signal strength with your GPS position, so you can see where your mesh works and where it drops out.

Live coverage map

Every reading lands on the map as you walk or drive, colored from strong to gone.

Estimated coverage

Fills in the gaps between your readings, and counts unanswered pings as no signal, so a dead zone looks dead.

Compare and test

Compare antennas, range settings and radios over the same route. Guided tests walk you through each step.

Base beacon and pings

The base sends a regular beacon and your phone pings it, so you learn both directions of the link.

CSV and KML export

Take your sessions to a spreadsheet or Google Earth. Exports go only where you send them.

One test, start to finish

The base on a tripod at the Cove Oasis trailhead in La Quinta. A drive through the Cove and out behind the east ridge, then the same drive with a better antenna. Strong close in, fading with distance, and gone where the hills get in the way.

Field Report

One card per session or comparison, ready to share in a chat or a forum post. The route is drawn without coordinates, and you can hide the area around your base. Pick what goes under it: a plain grid that keeps your streets private, or the street or terrain map when you want people to see where you went.

Screens from the app's demo mode. The readings are modelled from the real terrain and streets of La Quinta Cove, not recorded on an actual drive.

Which nodes

Meshurement™ works with Meshtastic® nodes and MeshCore companion nodes over Bluetooth, both free. You need two of the same kind: one for the base and one to carry. Node Setup puts both on a private test channel, so nothing you do goes out on the public mesh, and it saves a backup first so you can put each node back the way it was.
